Brand exploration · August 2026

ContactLog

Four directions for the ContactLogo mark, drawn from the identity the product already ships: cream, ink, gold, and a letterpress serif.  One rule runs through all four — grey is the generic contact, gold is the matched logo.

01

The Swap

A contact tile split on the diagonal.  Above the seam: the grey silhouette every address book shows today.  Below it: ink and a gold spark — the moment ContactLogo replaces the monogram with a real brand mark.

The most literal telling of the before/after, and it is already an app icon.

App icon ready Reads to 32 px
02

Card & Badge

A business card whose avatar spot holds a gold-ring badge.  “A clean, recognizable logo on every business card” as a picture.

The ringed circle is a direct evolution of the favicon already deployed at contactlogo.com, so this direction costs nothing in continuity.

Favicon continuity Reads to 16 px
03

C Aperture

A heavy letter C — ContactLogo’s monogram — whose open mouth receives a gold dot: the C accepting a logo.

The most abstract and most scalable of the four.  Legible at 16 px, works in one color, and doubles as favicon, watermark, or stamp.  Pairs naturally with the wordmark.

Primary candidate One-color Reads to 16 px
04

The Grid Lights Up

An address-book grid: three grey contacts, one lit in ink and gold.  Out of a book full of grey monograms, ContactLogo finds the brands.

Best at medium and large sizes — marketing pages, splash screens, empty states.  The person glyphs soften below 32 px, so it is a motif more than an icon.

Marketing motif Best at 48 px and up
—

Wordmark

ContactLog

ContactLog set in the product serif (Iowan Old Style / Palatino / Georgia), with the final o replaced by the gold-ring badge — the same badge as the favicon and Concept 02.  If adopted, the letterforms get converted to outlines so the wordmark does not depend on installed fonts.

—

Palette

Ink#1C1917
Cream#F5F0E8
Gold#C4A574
Grey#DDD6C9

Grey is always the unbranded contact; gold always marks the brand moment.  That keeps the logo telling the same story as the review screen, where confidence and overrides — not blind automation — do the work.

—

Recommendation

03 C Aperture as the primary mark — the most distinctive of the four, scales from favicon to splash screen, and survives one-color printing.

Pair it with the wordmark above for lockups, and keep the Concept 02 badge as the favicon it already is.  01 and 04 earn their keep as illustration motifs in onboarding, empty states, and the review screen even if they are not the logo.
